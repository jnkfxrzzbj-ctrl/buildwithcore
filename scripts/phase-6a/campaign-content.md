# November 2026–January 2027 campaign and content pack

## Position and workload

Lead with “YOUR PC. BUILT BY YOU.” Explain a small number of choices clearly and demonstrate how CORE checks them. Do not claim unique technology, laboratory testing, guaranteed performance, cheapest prices or an available full basket without evidence. All content is draft for owner publication; nothing has been posted or sent.

Default cadence: Tuesday and Friday, one 25–40 second original vertical video, reused on YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels; one supporting Instagram carousel every other week; one useful article every two weeks; one monthly newsletter after approval. Allocate 2–3 hours/week by batching two scripts/recordings. If research expands, reduce to one video rather than outsourcing unverified claims. Original footage can be narrated screen recordings of approved CORE pages, a written checklist or owner-shot hardware. No misleading AI assembly imagery or unrelated PC footage presented as CORE testing.

YouTube offers discoverability and reusable explanations, but Shorts link-out friction limits direct response: use profile links [S6]. Instagram offers saved/shared checklists and conversation; evaluate profile taps and qualified questions, not only views. Use the same source video without another platform's watermark. Do not assume follower growth converts to visits.

Reddit is a research and helpful-participation channel, not a scheduled promotional funnel. `r/buildapc` explicitly bans self-promotion/affiliate links; `r/buildmeapc` bans affiliate links [S14]. `r/buildapcforme` is for genuine requests using its template. Observe UK-specific questions and contribute useful non-promotional answers only when appropriate. No invented request posts, planted testimonials, repeated CORE links, automated replies or assumed “10% promotion allowance.” Recheck each community's current rules before participating; seek moderator permission for any proposed exceptional project-feedback post, with separate owner permission before contacting them.

1. 1–3 November: owner approves educational versus buying launch scope. Set up approved measurement, record release SHA and baseline. Check the exact public journey and profile link. If measurement is not approved, keep native platform results and mark website measures unavailable.
2. 3 and 6 November: publish scripts 1 and 2. Ask five genuine beginner volunteers to describe what CORE offers, what HOLD means and their next step. Record anonymous observations, not a recruited marketing list.
3. 10 and 13 November: publish scripts 3 and 4. Publish “How to check a gaming PC parts list before buying in the UK,” linked from FAQ/build pages after release approval.
4. 17 and 20 November: publish scripts 5 and 6. Review which questions recur. Prepare the Black Friday article and issue using current research, without making price promises.
5. 24 and 27 November: publish scripts 7 and 8. Publish “Black Friday PC parts: check the whole basket, not just the discount.” Send issue 1 only to confirmed subscribers after provider/privacy approval.

The five observations and publishing counts are controllable learning goals, not traffic forecasts. Avoid optimisation based on tiny percentage differences. After 8–10 videos inspect absolute qualified visits per hour of production and choose which channel deserves continued effort.

## Ten ready-to-use scripts

Use captions and plain checklist visuals. Spoken text below is complete; the final CTA is for owner-controlled profiles. Where a landing page is not approved, point to CORE's existing FAQ/Home, not an unpublished route. Each caption should include: **“CORE may earn commission from qualifying retailer purchases through links on its website. Recommendations come first.”** If content becomes a specific affiliate promotion, apply an upfront **“Ad – affiliate”** label and review the relevant platform/programme rules before publishing. No direct affiliate links are included in this pack.

### 1. A good part can still be a bad buy — 3 November

**Script:** “A good graphics card can still be a bad buy at the wrong price. At CORE, choosing the part and deciding whether to buy it are separate decisions. If the price evidence is missing or out of date, we won't turn that uncertainty into a green light. That's why you may see HOLD. It means check the buying status before spending. Your PC. Built by you. Find CORE through our profile.”

**Visual:** Approved HOLD panel; highlight its real wording. **Caption:** “The recommendation is about the part. The buying decision also needs current evidence.” **Measure:** profile taps and FAQ/build views. No price or performance claim.

### 2. The price that is missing from a parts list — 6 November

**Script:** “Before you compare two PC builds, check what each total includes. Is delivery included? Does it include a Windows licence? What about the monitor, keyboard and mouse? A parts total and the cost of a usable setup are different numbers. Write those extras down before choosing your budget. CORE's website explains the build and its current buying status. The link is on our profile.”

**Visual:** Checklist, not invented prices. **Caption:** “Compare like with like before calling a build cheaper.” **Measure:** saves and site visits.

### 3. The name on the box is not always enough — 10 November

**Script:** “Two components can have almost the same name and still be different products. Capacity, dimensions, connectors and included accessories can change between versions. Before ordering, compare the exact manufacturer model number with your parts list. If it doesn't match, stop and check. A similar product photo is not proof. CORE keeps exact component identity separate from retailer links. See the approach through our profile.”

**Visual:** Fiction-free labels ‘model / capacity / revision’; do not show a guessed MPN. **Caption:** “Check the exact model, not just the product family.” **Measure:** saves and questions.

### 4. Compatibility has more than one checkbox — 13 November

**Script:** “A matching CPU socket is only the start of PC compatibility. You also need to check the motherboard's CPU support and BIOS, the right memory type, space for the cooler and graphics card, and the required power connections. Use the exact manufacturer's documentation. CORE's goal is to make those checks understandable, with clear limits where something still needs verification. Find the current build notes through our profile.”

**Visual:** Five labelled checks. **Caption:** “A compatible-looking list still deserves an exact-model check.” **Measure:** article visits and useful questions. No claim all three builds physically tested.

### 5. Should you buy one part every payday? — 17 November

**Script:** “Buying PC parts one at a time can spread the spending, but think about when you'll be able to test them together. Return policies and time limits vary. A component sitting unopened for weeks may be harder to deal with if something is wrong. Before buying, check the seller's policy and make a realistic assembly plan. There isn't one answer for everyone. Start with the complete build and the timing.”

**Visual:** Purchase → delivery → testing calendar, no asserted legal deadlines. **Caption:** “Plan when you can test the whole system.” **Measure:** saves and substantive comments.

### 6. Why yesterday's price is not today's promise — 20 November

**Script:** “A price screenshot records a moment. It doesn't prove the same model is still in stock, that delivery costs the same, or that the offer is still available. CORE treats freshness as part of the evidence. Old or missing information should make the advice more cautious. Before buying, check the exact item, seller, stock and delivered total. Find CORE's current status through our profile.”

**Visual:** Real dated research note clearly labelled historical; no retailer promotion. **Caption:** “A timestamp matters. So does what was actually checked.” **Measure:** build visits.

### 7. Your Black Friday PC checklist — 24 November

**Script:** “Before Black Friday, write down the exact PC parts you need and your total budget. When a sale appears, check the model number, condition, seller, stock and delivery. Then check the rest of the basket. One discounted part doesn't make an incomplete or overpriced PC a good buy. If the evidence isn't there, waiting is a valid decision. Our buying approach is on CORE; use the link on our profile.”

**Visual:** Five checks plus complete basket. **Caption:** “Buy the build you need, not a discount badge.” **Measure:** saves/profile taps. No predicted discounts.

### 8. No deal is also a useful answer — 27 November

**Script:** “It's Black Friday, but the calendar doesn't make a PC good value. If we can't verify the exact parts, availability and total cost, we won't pretend we have a complete deal. You can still use the day to compare, prepare and learn what matters. Check CORE's current status before buying, and don't mistake an old target price for an offer available now. The website is linked on our profile.”

**Visual:** Current approved status only, no countdown. **Caption:** “A useful buying service must be able to say wait.” **Measure:** qualified visits and feedback, not forced click growth.

### 9. Before you open the first box — first December week

**Script:** “Before your first PC build, make space, gather the exact manuals and check that the delivered parts match your list. Keep packaging and order details. Read through the assembly sequence before starting, and check what you'll need for the operating system and first boot. If a guide shows different hardware, pause rather than guessing. CORE's guide pages explain their current scope and readiness. Start there through our profile.”

**Visual:** Owner-shot desk/checklist, no fake installed hardware. **Caption:** “Preparation is part of the build.” **Measure:** guide visits and saves.

### 10. January upgrade: start with the problem — first January week

**Script:** “Before buying a January upgrade, name the problem you're trying to solve. Are you running out of storage? Is a particular game struggling at your chosen settings? Or do you simply want a quieter PC? Those are different jobs. Check the evidence for your exact system before choosing a part, and include compatibility and total cost. An upgrade should solve your problem, not just carry a sale label.”

**Visual:** Storage / gaming workload / noise questions, no benchmark. **Caption:** “A clear problem makes a better shopping list.” **Measure:** saves, replies and return visits.

## Research-to-content process

Maintain one editorial item record: claim, exact component/configuration if relevant, primary source URL, evidence timestamp, delivery/stock scope, known uncertainty, owner approval, landing route, publication ID and next review trigger. A price observation may expire before a scheduled post: recheck immediately before publication, or remove the price and use evergreen education. Never reset the evidence timestamp because copy was rewritten.

One verified research finding becomes one article paragraph, one short script and one newsletter item. AI can draft from the record, but cannot turn uncertainty into a recommendation. Owner checks the claim and preview, then publishes manually/native scheduling. Record actual platform IDs and dates, UTM code, production minutes, visits and questions in the weekly log. If a claim becomes wrong, correct the destination and amend/remove the post where practical; document the correction. Retain source provenance for future agent use.
