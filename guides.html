From boxes to first boot

This is a general assembly checklist, not a physically tested walkthrough of a CORE build. First open your Starter checks, exact Performance notes or High-End checks. If the parts differ, stop and check their manuals.

  1. Prepare the space and parts

    Use a clear, dry table with good light, a Phillips screwdriver and a container for screws. Keep drinks away. Check model labels and inspect for damage. Leave the PSU unplugged during assembly. Handle boards by their edges and use anti-static precautions described in their manuals. Never open a PSU.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: the eight model labels match your saved build, the table is clear, the manuals are available and the PSU is unplugged.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: any item is damaged, missing or different from the specified model.

  2. Fit the processor

    Place the motherboard on its cardboard box, not on the outside of an anti-static bag. Open the socket using the board’s instructions. Match the CPU alignment mark and lower it gently into place. AM4 and AM5 use different retention systems. Do not touch pins or socket contacts, force the CPU, or remove socket parts unless the manual instructs you to.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: the CPU is seated and the socket retention is secured exactly as shown in your motherboard manual, without force.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: the alignment marks or socket mechanism are unclear. Do not press harder.

  3. Install memory and storage

    Use the recommended two-module slots in the exact board manual. Align each memory notch and press evenly until properly seated. Fit the M.2 SSD using the board’s correct standoff and retainer. Remove any heatsink thermal-pad protective film, but leave the SSD’s own label intact. Do not overtighten a small M.2 screw.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: both RAM modules are fully seated in the specified slots and the M.2 drive is secured with its heatsink correctly refitted.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: the memory notch does not align or you cannot identify the correct slots or retainer.

  4. Fit the cooler’s mounting hardware

    Use only the mounting kit for your CPU socket. Remove the cooler-base protective film if present. Follow the cooler’s paste instructions; do not add paste on top of pre-applied paste. Tighten evenly in the stated order. Keep the correct backplate and confirm memory clearance. Connect the CPU fan to the named CPU fan header. For liquid cooling, follow its separate radiator, pump and fan instructions; check clearances before mounting.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: the correct socket kit is fitted, protective film is removed, the cooler is secure and its fan is connected to the CPU fan header.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: the mounting kit, paste application, fan position or case/RAM clearance is uncertain.

  5. Install motherboard and PSU

    Check that every case standoff sits under a matching motherboard mounting hole; remove extras. Fit a separate I/O shield first if your board needs one. Lower the board without scraping it across standoffs. Use the supplied screws without overtightening. Orient the PSU intake towards the case ventilation, following the case manual.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: only matching standoffs are present, the board is securely mounted and the PSU is fitted according to the case manual.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: a standoff touches the underside of the board where there is no mounting hole.

  6. Connect power, case cables and fans

    Use the modular cables supplied for this exact PSU. Connect the 24-pin motherboard cable and the CPU/EPS power leads required by the board. EPS and PCIe GPU cables are different. Use the board’s header diagram for the power switch, USB and front audio. Check fan and hub power requirements. Never connect a 5V addressable RGB plug to a 12V RGB header.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: motherboard and CPU power, front-panel switches, USB/audio and case fans match the exact motherboard header diagrams.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: a plug or header cannot be positively identified. Never force or interchange EPS and GPU power connectors.

  7. Install the graphics card

    Use the slot specified in the hardware notes and board manual; it is not always the uppermost slot. Remove the matching case slot covers, seat the card, secure its bracket and fit its support if supplied. Fully insert the required GPU power connectors. Do not copy another configuration’s lead count: Option B’s ASUS card uses three separate PCIe leads; the proposed Reaper 9070 XT uses two.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: the GPU is fully seated in the specified slot, fixed to the case and connected with the exact required PSU leads.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: the GPU does not seat easily, power leads are uncertain or a cable obstructs a fan.

  8. Check, then power on

    Remove loose screws and packaging. Check that fans can turn freely, connectors are seated and cables cannot enter blades. Connect the monitor to the graphics card, plus a keyboard. Only then connect mains power and turn on. Initial memory training can take several minutes; avoid repeatedly cutting power. If there is smoke, a burning smell or an electrical fault, disconnect power safely and seek qualified help.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: no loose parts remain, fans can spin freely, the monitor is connected to the GPU and the first start shows normal signs of power.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: there is smoke, burning smell, electrical fault or a fan obstruction; disconnect power safely.

  9. Check firmware and basic operation

    Use the motherboard manual’s setup key. Confirm the CPU, memory capacity and SSD are detected, and that cooler fans or pump are operating. If CPU temperature rises rapidly, shut down and check cooling with power unplugged. Start with default memory settings. Only enable XMP/EXPO after basic operation is stable; profiles are not a guarantee. Use the exact board’s official BIOS instructions if an update is needed, and never interrupt an update.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: BIOS detects the expected CPU, RAM and SSD, and cooling operates without rapidly rising CPU temperature.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: the cooler is not working or the CPU temperature rises rapidly. Power down before investigating.

  10. Install the OS and test

    Follow the Windows route below. Install official motherboard chipset/network and graphics drivers, then system updates. Check everyday use and a game before changing performance settings. If a problem appears after enabling a memory profile, return to defaults. Close the case only after checking airflow and cable clearance.

    Check before moving on: Ready when: the intended OS drive is identified, installation completes, official drivers are installed and basic use is stable.
    Pause and get help: Stop if: the installer proposes erasing a drive you cannot confidently identify.
If the first boot does not work

Check the monitor input and graphics-card connection first. Read the board’s diagnostic lights and manual. Before touching internal parts, switch off and unplug. Recheck 24-pin, CPU power, GPU power, RAM seating and the front-panel switch connection. Make one change at a time. Do not bridge unfamiliar pins or keep powering a system with an electrical fault.

Install Windows

Windows is separate from the hardware price. Downloading the installer does not provide a licence. Use a valid licence for the edition you install.

On another suitable Windows PC, use Microsoft’s Create Windows 11 Installation Media instructions ↗ to prepare a blank USB drive of at least 8GB. The process erases that USB drive. For other computers, follow Microsoft’s available ISO guidance.

Boot the new PC from the USB using the motherboard’s documented boot menu. Follow Microsoft’s setup instructions. Confirm the destination drive carefully: deleting partitions or formatting erases data. Disconnect unrelated storage before installation if you cannot identify it confidently. Afterwards, install official drivers and updates.

Linux is an optional route. Check support for your games, anti-cheat software and exact hardware before choosing it. This release does not provide a validated Linux walkthrough.

Keep it useful

Keep your parts list, manuals and receipts. Back up important files and keep the operating system and drivers updated. With the PC switched off and unplugged, clean accessible dust filters according to the case instructions. Keep liquids away and never open the PSU.

Upgrade to solve a specific problem: more storage for a full drive, more memory for a workload that needs it, or a graphics card for demanding games. Check power, space, BIOS and compatibility again before changing a part. Some upgrades also need a new platform; there is no promise that every future CPU will fit. Many prebuilts can be maintained and upgraded too.
